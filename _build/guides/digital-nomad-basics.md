Working from anywhere sounds simple: laptop, flight, done. The people who make it last for years treat it as a work setup to design, not a holiday with a laptop. Here's what to sort out before you go.

## Is it right for your work style?

Location-independent work suits some styles better than others:

- **[Scouts](../styles/scout.html)** and **[Catalysts](../styles/catalyst.html)** often love the variety.
- **[Sprinters](../styles/sprinter.html)** do well if they keep a tight routine.
- **[Cartographers](../styles/cartographer.html)** usually prefer "slow travel" — a month or more per place — because moving every few days breaks their focus.

[Take the assessment](../quiz.html) to see where you land.

## 1. Internet

Your connection is your office. Before booking accommodation, ask for a speed-test screenshot. For video calls, aim for a stable connection rather than the fastest headline number. Always carry a backup: a local SIM or eSIM with data, and a list of nearby coworking spaces.

## 2. Time zones

Moving away from your team's time zone is the most common source of friction. Decide in advance:

- Your **overlap hours** with your team or clients
- Which meetings you'll attend live and which you'll watch recorded
- How you'll show your current time zone (calendar settings, chat status)

## 3. Visas and tax

This is the part people skip — and shouldn't. Rules vary by country and change often:

- Many countries now offer dedicated digital-nomad or remote-work visas
- Working on a tourist visa may not be permitted, even for a foreign employer
- Spending long periods in one country can affect where you owe tax

Check official government sources for every country you plan to work from, and talk to a tax professional who understands cross-border work. Your employer may also have rules about where you're allowed to work from.

## 4. Insurance

Standard travel insurance often excludes long trips or working abroad. Look for policies designed for long-term travellers or remote workers, and check what's covered for health care, equipment theft and trip interruption.

## 5. Gear

Travel light, but don't skimp on:

- A laptop stand and compact keyboard (your neck will thank you — see the [ergonomic checklist](home-office-setup.html))
- Noise-cancelling headphones with a good microphone
- A universal adapter and a short extension lead
- A backup charger and cloud backups for everything

## 6. Routine

The freedom is real; so is the risk of never quite working or never quite resting. A few anchors help:

- Same start ritual everywhere
- Work blocks before exploring
- A weekly review every Sunday or Monday
- One "admin day" per month for bills, bookings and paperwork

## 7. Community

Working alone in a new city every month gets lonely. Coworking spaces, coliving houses and local meetups are the easiest way to meet people with the same lifestyle.

## A simple first trip

If you've never done it, try a two- to four-week trip to a place in a similar time zone, with a coworking space nearby. You'll learn more about what you need from one month than from any guide.
