Asynchronous communication means communicating without expecting an immediate reply: a written update, a recorded walkthrough, a comment on a document. Done well, it replaces a large share of meetings and gives everyone more time to focus. Done badly, it creates slow threads full of follow-up questions.

## When to go async — and when not to

| Use async for | Use a live conversation for |
|---|---|
| Status updates | Sensitive or emotional topics |
| Proposals and feedback on documents | Conflict between people |
| Questions that aren't urgent | Brainstorming from a blank page |
| Decisions with clear options | Decisions stuck after two rounds of comments |
| Sharing information across time zones | Building new relationships |

A good rule: if a thread has gone back and forth more than three times without progress, book a short call — then write up the result.

## The anatomy of a good async message

1. **The ask in the first line.** "Need your approval on the Q3 budget by Thursday."
2. **Context.** What happened, what you've considered, links to the relevant documents.
3. **Options.** If there's a decision, lay out the choices and your recommendation.
4. **A default.** "If I don't hear back by Thursday 17:00, I'll go with option B."
5. **Formatting.** Short paragraphs, bullet points, bold for the key line.

## Templates

### Weekly update

> **Shipped:** what's done this week
> **In progress:** what's moving, with expected dates
> **Blocked:** what's stuck and who can unblock it
> **Next week:** top three priorities

### Decision request

> **Decision needed:** one sentence
> **By when:** date and time with time zone
> **Options:** A, B, C — with one line on the trade-off of each
> **Recommendation:** which option and why
> **Default if no reply:** what you'll do

### Handoff

> **What this is:** one-sentence summary
> **Where things are:** links
> **What's left:** checklist
> **Watch out for:** known risks
> **Who to ask:** names

## Setting team norms

Async only works when everyone shares expectations. Agree, in writing:

- **Response times** by channel (for example: chat within four working hours, email within one business day)
- **What counts as urgent** and how to flag it (a phone call, not an @channel)
- **Where decisions are recorded** — one place, searchable
- **Core overlap hours** if the team spans time zones

## Async by work style

- **[Cartographers](../styles/cartographer.html)** and **[Tinkerers](../styles/tinkerer.html)** usually love async — it protects focus. The risk is going silent; a short weekly update fixes that.
- **[Collaborators](../styles/collaborator.html)** and **[Catalysts](../styles/catalyst.html)** can find async slow. Short recorded videos and scheduled live sessions keep their energy in the loop.
- **[Coordinators](../styles/coordinator.html)** are natural async champions — give them ownership of the team's norms.

## Measure the win

Before rolling out async norms, count your team's meeting hours for one week. Check again after a month. Use the [meeting cost calculator](../tools/meeting-cost.html) to put a number on the time you've given back.
